## Jev experiment — purpose, decisions, first findings, and next steps (as of 29 September 2026; next steps revised 2 October 2026)

This section is the boot point for the staging Jev experiment on `feature/mts-lane`. Production (`main`, `28fdd29`, source pack v5) is unaffected and stays the baseline.

### Purpose and target
- The owner's question is whether Jev (TypeSafe's cheap structured-classification model, a paid direct API) can add value as a judge in the selection loop compared with the deterministic keyword gate. It is a learning exercise first and possible next-stage development second; it is not production-ready.
- Target shape (owner's "judge fast, write smart" diagram): a cheap judge scores every collected candidate before the rules, rules and validation still decide, the large writer only sees survivors, and every judge score is logged beside the gate decision and publish outcome. Fail-open is required if any judge is down. The later "Rules pick. Jev is on trial" diagram is the accurate current state: only the rules can change an edition; Jev and the reranker are shadow-only; the dashed line from Jev to the selection rules is a hypothesis.
- Two differences between that diagram and the code matter. The diagram's judge is the Workers AI reranker plus embedding model with "no outside vendors"; Jev is a third-party API and would need its own fail-open rule and data-flow decision before promotion. And the writer already sees at most 18 pre-filtered candidates, so a judge saves almost no writer cost: any value has to come from selection quality (recall of good stories the keyword gate drops, removal of junk that slips through, or ordering), not spend.

### Design decisions made
- Jev's counterfactual pick is rank-based: the top K stories by `reader_wants`, where K is the gate's selection size. The reranker's pick is the top K by raw relevance. The comparison that decides the experiment is Jev against the free reranker as well as against the rules: if a paid vendor cannot beat the free on-platform judge the question is closed. The earlier four-threshold AND rule is retained only in the legacy verdict code.
- Only stories the picks disagree on can separate judges, and scores are packed into a narrow band, so a story a few hundredths either side of the Jev cut is tie-breaking, not disagreement. Each story's distance from the cut (the midpoint of the K-th and (K+1)-th score) is recorded; disagreements with a margin of 0.08 or more are reported as "clear", and disagreement cells are drawn most-decisive-first.
- The paired sample is up to 4 stories from each disagreement side, 1 anchor from each agreement side, and up to 2 repeats of stories labelled at least three days earlier (so repeats measure consistency, not memory); spare places fill round-robin. Within a disagreement side, stories clearly away from the Jev cut are drawn first, at random, and near-ties only fill what clear ones cannot; drawing the most extreme first was rejected because it samples the places where Jev looks best. The dropped pool is up to 40 unjudged rules-dropped stories and reports whether it is a full census.
- The paired sample and the dropped pool are separate frames, because in the dropped pool the owner knows every story was rejected. A story is voted once per frame, the frames are counted apart and never pooled, and a story labelled in one can still be offered in the other. Pooling them (an earlier version of this change) was rejected after review: it mixed two labelling conditions and could only ever add Jev-favoured stories. Every label records the judge picks and cell computed over its whole run; earlier labels get their picks back from the run's retained shadow report. Labels are append-only; saves are guarded against double-counting.
- Labelling is blind: the open-batch API withholds scores, gate outcome, judge picks and sample group; they appear only after saving. "Unsure" is reported both excluded and read as "would not add".
- Jev scores live in a durable `jev_judgments` ledger keyed by story and question fingerprint (which ignores the daily prior-title list). Shadow reports remain pruned to 15 rows. Analysis currently reads scores from each label's snapshot, so comparing question sets on the same labels needs a join by story and question hash first.
- An independent review (Fable) of this change found and drove fixes for: an unscored reranker story being counted as a reject vote, pooling of the two labelling frames, the extreme-first draw, a bar that a coin flip could pass, and unrecorded inconsistencies between analyses. Its remaining caveats stand: the sample is drawn from disagreements, so the sign test and AUC hold within the sample and compare judges rather than give absolute accuracy; roughly 130 clear disagreements would be needed to show a judge worth deploying beats 65% with confidence, so the 40-decision bar is a first look, not a deployment gate.
- Ranking the publish set is a separate optional step. The earlier binary disagreement queue is retired from the page; its endpoints and rows remain as legacy data.
- The bar, confirmed by the owner on 29 September 2026, is computed in the analysis panel: (1) the owner agrees with their own repeats on at least 75% of at least 10; (2) Jev is right on at least 65% of at least 40 clear disagreements with the rules; (3) Jev is right significantly more often than the reranker (exact sign test p below 0.10) on at least 20 stories where they differ; a tie is not a pass. Any change should be made before results are read, not after. A judge with no score for a story is excluded for that story, not counted as saying no.

### Ideas raised and challenged (kept for later)
- Judge the ceiling before the judge: label what the rules drop without Jev's scores. If the owner would rescue almost nothing, the value is junk removal and ranking, not recall.
- A daily counterfactual (edition from rules versus edition from Jev ranking on the same pool) labelled only on the symmetric difference is a stronger design than random samples; the paired sample approximates it.
- Score backfill of published editions would give a precision-only baseline; excluded stories from earlier days are not recoverable.
- A stronger model as a proxy labeller would only measure agreement with that model, not the owner; usable for triage only.
- Question experiments once enough labels exist: a sharper or graded `reader_wants`, and the owner's own labelled stories embedded as examples in the question state (a new question-set version compared with `reader-want-v1` on the same labels). The `substantive` question conflicts with the owner's taste and is not part of the pick rule.
- Not yet resolved: the third-party data flow (titles and summaries to TypeSafe) and fail-open behaviour are promotion questions, not needed while staging-only.

### First findings (19 labelled stories, one day; not conclusions; superseded by the 2 October labelled-data readout, which contradicts the low recall ceiling below)
- Where the rules and Jev's top picks disagreed, Jev was right on 2 of 7 decided (about 29%; the rules 5 of 7). With 7 decisions this is indistinguishable from a coin flip; roughly 30 decided disagreements are needed to separate 29% from 50%.
- Rules-dropped stories: the owner would rescue 0 of 6 decided (1 unsure), on one day. This points to a low recall ceiling.
- `reader_wants` AUC about 0.66 and reranker AUC about 0.68 over 14 votes: both weak and not distinguishable; the reranker's normalized scores are mostly near zero, so its AUC is fragile. Rank correlation (4 items), anchors (1 each), and repeats (none yet) are not informative.
- Story-level: `reader_wants` is compressed (mostly 0.4–0.66, one story above 0.8), so the top-K cut falls in a dense zone; the one Jev-only story the owner would publish scored 0.80 under a profile interest weighted 0; 5 of 19 labels were "unsure".

## 4 October 2026 — headroom test designed and piloted (no runtime change; tooling outside the repository)

- Why: the open question under the whole judge experiment is how far the rules' edition sits from the owner's own ideal edition. Three judges and an enforced review loop exist, but no number for that gap and no trivial baseline (recency or random). The test is a blind pick: for a day's candidate pool the owner chooses the stories they would publish, then the pick is compared with the rules' selection, the newest stories, and the Jev and reranker top-k.
- Finding that reshaped the plan (read-only, no scores read): of ten retained shadow runs with judge scores, the 1 to 4 October pools are almost entirely labelled by the owner already (17 to 23 labelled stories in pools of 20 to 31), so a blind pick from them would not be blind, and consecutive runs share 65 to 88% of their stories, so the ten runs hold about four independent days. The 30 September pools are clean (2 to 3 labelled) but the rules selected 18 of about 30 stories there, which makes an overlap partly chance. The design is therefore a pilot on the 30 September 08:15 UTC pool (33 stories, run `80eaf092`) and then one fresh pool per day, exported before the owner labels it.
- Built in `~/Projects/ai-signal-headroom` (not in this repository): `export.py` writes a blind sheet and a sealed key, `picker.html` is the picking page, `analyze.py` computes the pre-registered metrics and refuses to run if a sealed key changed. The metrics, thresholds (rules recall of at least 0.80 is little headroom, 0.60 to 0.80 some, below 0.60 large) and the test-retest rule are in that folder's `README.md`, written before any pick. `analyze.py` was tested only on synthetic data. Clef scores are deliberately not read, because the Clef comparison stays locked until its own bar opens.
- Sealed pilot key `P1`: SHA-256 `78e29c852e5e93a5023f611edb27f5bbf2aa5859316d964dad4890a1d018ec09`. The key's contents have not been printed or read.
- Pilot result (P1, one pool, run `80eaf092`: profile v3, source pack v7, pool 33, the rules selected 18, the owner picked 14, which is the picker's cap). The sealed key matched its fingerprint before it was opened. Primary metrics: rules recall 0.71 (10 of the 14 picks) and precision 0.56 (10 of 18); the 14 newest stories 0.43; Jev top 14 0.50; reranker top 14 0.36; random 0.42; the pre-registered label is "some" headroom. Amendment 1, added after this result was read and stated as post hoc: at the rules' own size of 18 the rules catch 0.71, Jev 0.64 (9 of 14), recency 0.50, reranker 0.50, random 0.55, so no judge beat the rules and the gap to Jev is one story. Exploratory and not pre-registered: the four picks the rules dropped were AMD acquiring World Labs, an architecture opinion piece, a GPT-6 Astra safety-simulation story and DeepSeek V4 Flash costs; Jev ranked two of them (ranks 5 and 9 of 33) in its top 10 and the reranker none, which is the only hint of the rescue-layer hypothesis and rests on four stories. Limits: one pool; the pick hit the 14 cap so the true ideal may be larger; the pool is from the K=18 era and not today's v5 rules, which select 8 to 12; and the owner's pick has no test-retest yet. Nothing here supports changing selection.
- Pending: a P1 re-pick after at least three days for the test-retest overlap, then one fresh pool per day for about five days, each exported before it is labelled in /admin, and one re-pick after at least three days for the test-retest overlap. The export used `wrangler d1 execute` as read-only SELECTs, an "ask" action under the new governance table, run with the owner's approval of this work.

### Architectural guardrails

- The deterministic collector, not the model, materialises the story inventory.
- The model cannot introduce stories or source URLs.
- Editorial corroboration is never described as proof.
- AInews, TLDR AI, AlphaSignal, MTS Situations, and AI Brief enter one equal editorial pool; no source receives seniority.
- One failed or quiet source does not block a usable pool from the others.
- Prefer the first 36 hours; use 48 hours normally and expand once to 72 hours only when fewer than 10 qualified, deduplicated candidates remain. Never admit older material or weaken evidence/relevance rules to fill a target.
- X/Twitter is background noise and cannot become a published card or corroborating source.
- Quiet days remain quiet; do not pad to a story target.
- Failed runs cannot replace the last good edition.
- Normal refresh remains idempotent.
- Forced republishing remains owner-only and once per successful Melbourne day.
- `/admin` remains absent from public navigation.
- Historical failed-run rows remain immutable audit evidence.
